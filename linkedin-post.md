Fundraise Up just launched an AI Visibility Assessment for nonprofits, and the signal is right: donors are starting to ask AI assistants where to give before they ever land on a nonprofit website.

Their framing is useful because it moves AI from a vague communications concern to an operational question: when an assistant summarizes your organization, does it find the right facts, cite the right pages, and recommend you for the right reasons?

I built a free companion tool for teams that want to inspect that problem themselves:

GEO Audit Studio
https://wayan.com/geo-audit/

It generates a structured audit brief you can paste into ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Claude, or another browsing AI tool. It tests whether AI assistants can find, understand, cite, and accurately represent a public website.

I added two presets:

Nonprofit AI Visibility Audit
For development, communications, and executive teams that need to know how AI describes their mission, programs, donation routes, impact evidence, and peer alternatives.

Funder AI Accuracy Audit
For foundations and grantmakers that need to know whether AI assistants accurately describe funding priorities, eligibility, exclusions, geography, deadlines, grant sizes, and application routes to grantseekers.

That second use case matters. If AI tools misstate a foundation's priorities, the cost is not abstract. Grantseekers waste time. Program staff absorb avoidable inquiries. Ineligible applications increase. Public guidance looks inconsistent even when the real problem is that the source material is scattered or stale.

The tool also includes a one-screen executive result mode for board or leadership review, plus scorecard, measurement, and action-plan modules.

This does not claim to reveal private AI rankings. It gives social-impact teams a transparent way to test public AI answers, find the weak source pages, and fix the facts that shape donor, applicant, journalist, and partner decisions before their next content update.
